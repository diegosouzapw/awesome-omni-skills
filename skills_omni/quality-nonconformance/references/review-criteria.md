# Review Criteria for Nonconformance, CAPA, Supplier Escalation, and Closure

Use this matrix during live packet review. It is a decision aid, not a substitute for approved procedures.

## 1) Minimum evidence to accept an NCR as reviewable

Before deeper judgment, confirm the packet identifies:

- product/process affected
- lot, batch, serial, or population scope
- detection point
- current material status and location
- distribution status
- preliminary risk statement
- assigned owner and disposition authority

If two or more of these are missing, the record is not ready for meaningful closure review.

## 2) Disposition review matrix

| Disposition path | Accept when | Red flags |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hold for investigation | Scope or cause is not yet reliable, and product control is maintained | Material status unclear; mixed with conforming stock; no owner |
| Use as is | Nonconformance is fully characterized, impact is acceptable, and authority is defined by procedure | Convenience decision; no documented rationale; safety/performance impact not assessed |
| Rework / repair | Rework method is defined, controlled, and followed by required reinspection or reevaluation | Rework performed without approved method; no reevaluation evidence |
| Return to supplier | Defect is supplier-linked and traceable, with receiving and supplier records aligned | Used as default without checking internal control failure |
| Scrap | Material is clearly unsuitable and segregation/disposition is documented | Scrap chosen to avoid investigation of recurrence |
| Concession / deviation | Temporary acceptance is authorized, documented, and risk-justified | Repeated concessions masking a chronic process issue |

## 3) NCR may remain local when most of the following are true

- isolated event with no meaningful trend signal
- low or contained risk
- no distributed product impact
- cause is supported by direct evidence
- correction is local and proportionate
- no prior similar event suggesting system weakness
- supplier history does not show repetition
- effectiveness can be confirmed without full CAPA infrastructure

If these conditions are not met, test for CAPA escalation.

## 4) CAPA escalation triggers

Escalate or require explicit non-escalation rationale when any of the following is present:

- recurrence after prior correction
- defect escaped to customer, complaint, service, or field use
- potential safety, efficacy, or critical-performance impact
- multiple lots, lines, or sites may be affected
- detectability failure or weak inspection/control method
- validation failure, drift, or loss of controlled state
- supplier repeat issue or ineffective previous SCAR
- management-review visibility is warranted due to risk or trend
- prior closure proved ineffective
- root cause points to a broader system or process weakness

A weak packet says, "No CAPA required," without showing why these triggers do not apply.

## 5) Supplier escalation checkpoints

Open or intensify supplier action when review shows one or more of these conditions:

- repeat defect from the same supplier or process step
- defect in a critical material, component, or outsourced operation
- specification, drawing, or acceptance criteria mismatch
- supplier change not disclosed or not assessed
- incoming inspection repeatedly failed to detect supplier escapes
- quality agreement obligations were not met
- supplier corrective action history is ineffective or overdue

Do not assume supplier-only root cause until internal specification clarity, incoming controls, and communication quality are also checked.

## 6) Root-cause evidence quality scale

### Weak
- conclusion based mainly on interview or assumption
- cause selected because it is plausible, not demonstrated
- "operator error" or "supplier issue" with no corroborating records
- no review of history, changes, or control effectiveness

### Adequate
- conclusion supported by records, timing, and elimination of major alternatives
- history and trend checks performed
- containment scope aligns with evidence
- contributing factors documented if present

### Strong
- direct evidence ties failure mechanism to the identified cause
- competing hypotheses were tested or ruled out
- control weakness is identified, not just immediate failure
- action plan clearly addresses the verified cause and detectability gap

## 7) Closure evidence expectations

A closure is usually credible only if it includes all of the following:

- implementation evidence for each committed action
- updated records, procedures, specifications, or controls where applicable
- verification or validation where required by the action
- effectiveness metric or review window defined in advance
- evidence from that review window
- assessment of unintended consequences where risk justifies it
- approval by the appropriate authority level

## 8) Reopen criteria

Reopen or reject closure when any of the following occurs:

- same or closely related defect recurs
- implementation happened, but effectiveness was never checked
- new evidence contradicts the claimed root cause
- additional affected scope is discovered after closure
- supplier or process changes invalidate the original assumptions
- disposition or release decisions cannot be reconstructed from the record

## 9) Common review traps

- Containment described vaguely: "material quarantined" without quantity, location, or traceability.
- Root cause overstated from limited evidence.
- Training used as the only corrective action for a process-capability problem.
- Supplier blamed without checking internal specifications or receiving controls.
- CAPA closed on completion date instead of effectiveness evidence.
- Concession or deviation repeatedly used to normalize chronic defects.

## 10) Quick reviewer prompts

Use these prompts when the packet feels incomplete:

- What exactly was affected, and where is it now?
- What evidence shows the chosen disposition was authorized and appropriate?
- What evidence supports the claimed root cause over other plausible causes?
- Why is this not a CAPA, given the observed risk or recurrence?
- If the supplier is involved, what internal control failed to catch or prevent the issue?
- What evidence proves the fix worked beyond initial implementation?
- If this happens again next month, what in the record would let us say the closure was still reasonable?
